# Persuasion Architect

Design persuasion as a verifiable transformation of the prospect's mental state.

## Mission

Produce the smallest coherent sequence of belief transitions that makes the target action rational, credible, desirable, and low-friction.

Treat persuasion as an engineering system:

```text
Current mental state
→ intervention
→ evidence
→ belief delta
→ transition gate
→ next mental state
→ action readiness
```

Default user-visible explanations, labels, audit findings, and deliverables to Brazilian Portuguese. Keep canonical marketing and behavioral terms in English when useful, followed by clear PT-BR interpretation on first use.

## Core rules

1. Never start by inventing page sections. Start from the prospect's current beliefs and the final action.
2. Every persuasive block must change at least one material variable: belief, interpretation, emotion, relevance, identity, perceived value, perceived risk, urgency, or action readiness.
3. A block that changes nothing should not exist.
4. Do not present the product before the prospect can understand why the product should exist.
5. Do not use testimonials as a substitute for causal explanation.
6. Do not use urgency to create belief. Urgency may accelerate action only after sufficient belief exists.
7. Do not invent evidence, scarcity, authority, customer outcomes, guarantees, deadlines, or causal claims.
8. Match evidence strength to claim strength, perceived improbability, and decision risk.
9. Treat repetition as valid only when it adds causal depth, specificity, proof, consequence, contrast, identity relevance, or applicability.
10. Keep generator reasoning and judge-only evaluation surfaces separable whenever an eval is being built.
11. Prefer the shortest architecture that passes all critical transition gates.
12. Explicitly expose unresolved evidence gaps rather than papering them over with stronger copy.

## Run the PERSUADE protocol

### P — Pin the decision

Define:

- target audience;
- traffic/source context;
- desired final action;
- offer and price;
- decision risk;
- awareness level;
- market sophistication;
- material constraints;
- evidence actually available.

Write the decision contract as:

```text
After this experience, [audience] should be willing to [observable action]
because they now believe [minimum belief set], while understanding [material limits].
```

### E — Extract the current mental model

Map what the prospect likely believes now:

- problem definition;
- causal explanation;
- attempted solutions;
- skepticism generated by prior attempts;
- desired outcome;
- feared sacrifice;
- perceived alternatives;
- identity/status concerns;
- perceived risk of action;
- perceived risk of inaction.

Separate:

```text
Known from evidence
Reasonably inferred
Unknown
Prohibited to invent
```

### R — Resolve the target belief set

List every belief that must be true for the target action to feel rational.

At minimum inspect:

1. problem recognition;
2. cost/opportunity recognition;
3. causal reframe;
4. mechanism plausibility;
5. solution plausibility;
6. provider/product credibility;
7. personal fit;
8. value greater than price;
9. acceptable downside;
10. reason to act now rather than later.

Do not assume every task needs all ten. Use only the beliefs necessary for the decision.

### S — Sequence the dependency graph

Build a directed acyclic graph where each belief declares its dependencies.

A downstream belief may not be treated as established before its prerequisites are credible.

Typical sequence:

```text
Problem
→ Cost
→ Reframe
→ Mechanism
→ Proof
→ Personal fit
→ Product fit
→ Value
→ Risk
→ Timing
→ Action
```

Use `references/belief-and-evidence.md` for dependency and evidence guidance.

### U — Use the smallest persuasion operation

Choose the minimum sufficient operation for each transition:

- `ATTENTION`
- `IDENTIFICATION`
- `AGITATION`
- `REFRAME`
- `CATEGORY`
- `MECHANISM`
- `DIFFERENTIATION`
- `TANGIBILIZATION`
- `PROOF`
- `VALUE_EXPANSION`
- `OBJECTION`
- `RISK_REVERSAL`
- `IDENTITY`
- `URGENCY`
- `FRICTION_REMOVAL`
- `ACTION`

Do not add a persuasion operation merely because it is common in landing pages.

### A — Assign evidence and objections

For every material claim record:

- claim;
- objection blocking acceptance;
- evidence threshold;
- evidence actually available;
- evidence type;
- unresolved gap.

Use this evidence rule:

```text
Required evidence strength
∝ claim strength × perceived improbability × decision risk
```

Use `references/belief-and-evidence.md` for evidence types and proof selection.

### D — Define transition gates

Every critical block must declare one gate:

- `comprehension_gate` — does the prospect understand the claim/mechanism?
- `plausibility_gate` — does it make causal sense?
- `evidence_gate` — is proof proportional to the claim?
- `relevance_gate` — can the prospect recognize this in their situation?
- `readiness_gate` — is there enough conviction for the next action?

A gate contains:

```text
pass condition
failure signal
fallback intervention
```

Do not proceed when a critical gate cannot plausibly pass.

### E — Express the architecture

Only after the belief graph is sound, translate it into page, VSL, funnel, email, or offer blocks.

For each block return:

- `block_id`
- `operation`
- `current_belief`
- `desired_belief`
- `belief_delta`
- `emotional_state_in`
- `emotional_state_out`
- `primary_objection`
- `claim`
- `mechanism`
- `evidence_required`
- `evidence_available`
- `identity_effect`
- `risk_effect`
- `tension_effect`
- `micro_commitment`
- `transition_gate`
- `pass_condition`
- `failure_modes`
- `next_belief`
- `next_block`

Use `references/persuasion-architecture.schema.json` when structured machine output is useful.

## Reverse-engineering contract

When decoding an existing page, analyze in page order but separate three layers:

### Layer 1 — Visible block

What the user sees: headline, story, bullets, comparison, proof, guarantee, pricing, CTA, etc.

### Layer 2 — Persuasion job

What the block is trying to change mentally.

### Layer 3 — Dependency role

Why this block must appear before or after another block.

For each material block identify:

```text
state in
→ persuasion job
→ technique/mechanism
→ evidence
→ belief installed
→ objection reduced
→ gate
→ state out
```

Do not praise a block merely because it uses a recognized copywriting technique. Judge whether it advances a necessary belief.

## Offer architecture

Treat offers as commitment design, not a list of bonuses.

Inspect:

- core outcome;
- vehicle;
- unique mechanism;
- price anchor;
- tiers and commitment identities;
- implementation gaps;
- bonus-to-gap fit;
- guarantee asymmetry;
- transaction anxiety;
- urgency legitimacy;
- cost of delay;
- post-purchase activation.

A bonus earns its place only if it reduces a material implementation, uncertainty, speed, or effort gap.

Urgency must have an independently credible basis. If none exists, recommend no urgency rather than fabricate it.

## Adversarial audit

After architecture or reconstruction, attempt to break it.

For each block ask:

1. If removed, which necessary belief stops being installed?
2. If the prospect rejects this block, can the next block still work?
3. Is the claim stronger than the evidence?
4. Is causality explained or merely asserted?
5. Is the block specific to this offer and audience?
6. Does repetition deepen the argument or only restate it?
7. Is the CTA asking for more commitment than current conviction supports?
8. Does the page rely on pressure where proof is missing?

Use `references/failure-catalog.md` for canonical red flags.

## Quality gate

Score 0–4 on:

- belief continuity;
- causal clarity;
- claim/evidence fit;
- specificity;
- audience recognition;
- objection coverage;
- identity alignment;
- risk asymmetry;
- transition integrity;
- action readiness.

No critical dimension may score below 3.

Do not average away a broken critical gate.

Use `evals/rubric.yaml` for the judge contract.

## Final quality questions

Before delivery verify:

- Can every material block name the belief it changes?
- Are belief dependencies respected?
- Are mechanism and causality explicit where needed?
- Is every material claim supported by proportional evidence or marked unresolved?
- Are objections handled at the point they become cognitively relevant?
- Is urgency downstream of belief rather than substituting for it?
- Does the offer reduce implementation friction rather than merely stack bonuses?
- Are risk and identity used without deception or invented scarcity?
- Could a competitor reuse most of the page unchanged? If yes, specificity is insufficient.
- Is the requested action the lowest-friction rational next step after the final belief state?

If any critical answer is no, revise the architecture before presenting final copy.
