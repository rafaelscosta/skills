# Mechanism Contract

Use this reference whenever a persuasive claim depends on why an intervention should produce an outcome.

A named framework, category, or feature is not a mechanism by itself.

## Minimum causal chain

For every material efficacy or outcome claim, model:

```text
starting condition
→ intervention
→ immediate change
→ intermediate behavior/state
→ expected outcome
```

If any arrow is unknown, mark it unknown. Do not bridge the gap with rhetoric.

## Required mechanism fields

Record:

- `starting_condition` — what is true before intervention;
- `intervention` — what actually changes or is done;
- `immediate_change` — the first observable effect;
- `intermediate_state` — the behavior/process state expected to change next;
- `expected_outcome` — the bounded result the mechanism could plausibly influence;
- `scope_conditions` — where the chain is expected to hold;
- `prerequisites` — what must already be true;
- `alternative_causes` — material rival explanations;
- `confounders` — factors that could produce the same observed result;
- `falsifier` — what observation would weaken the proposed mechanism;
- `evidence_state` — what is known, inferred, untested, or unsupported.

## Mechanism gate

A mechanism-dependent downstream claim may advance only if the reader can answer:

1. What specifically changes?
2. Through which intermediate state should that change affect the result?
3. Why is that intermediate state relevant to the claimed outcome?
4. Under what conditions might the chain fail?
5. What evidence supports the chain versus only the final outcome?

If those answers are unavailable, fail closed.

The fallback is one of:

- reduce the claim to a descriptive product/process claim;
- request or gather the missing mechanism information;
- downgrade the CTA to exploration, preview, diagnosis, or information gathering;
- explicitly state that efficacy remains unproven.
## Distinguish mechanism from adjacent evidence

Do not confuse:

- **artifact proof** with efficacy: a template can exist without improving results;
- **authority proof** with efficacy: credentials do not establish outcome effects;
- **case evidence** with mechanism proof: an outcome can occur for several reasons;
- **self-evidence** with causality: recognition establishes relevance, not cause;
- **category naming** with explanation: a memorable label does not explain an arrow.

Prefer evidence that directly interrogates the chain:

```text
demonstration of intervention
+ observed intermediate change
+ outcome measurement
+ bounded context
```

When only outcome evidence exists, acknowledge plausible confounders.

## Causal clarity score floor

A material mechanism should not score 3/4 unless:

- the intervention is concrete;
- at least one intermediate state is explicit;
- the connection from intermediate state to outcome is explained;
- scope/prerequisites are bounded;
- material alternative causes are not silently erased.

Score 4/4 only when the mechanism also includes meaningful falsifiers, contrast with alternatives, and evidence targeted at causal links.
